Why I built it

AI makes more.
Someone has to run it.

AI tools let one person produce what used to take a crew. The catch is the paperwork nobody sees: dozens of clips per project, credits spread across several accounts, prompts that worked once and got lost, and a manager who just wants to know what is done and what it cost. I wanted a system that answers those questions in seconds, and that looks like it belongs in a production office.

01Where is everything?

A board from brief to delivery, deadlines for the next three weeks, and a list of what needs my decision today.

02What did it cost?

Each tool's monthly price in ringgit, credits per shot, credits lost to redos, and cost per finished minute of video.

03Can we repeat it?

Every shot keeps its exact prompt and version. Characters and brand rules are locked in a vault so the next project matches.

Shown with real work: the MILO spec ad, the Hassan animated shorts and the MECA newsletter release.

01

Board

Every project moves through seven stages, from brief to delivered. Each card carries a key frame, a project code, priority and how many shots are approved.

  • Drag a card to move it, or tap Next stage
  • Delivery date is stamped automatically
  • Shot progress bar shows kept against redone clips
02

Shot log

Every AI clip is logged like a camera report: the scene, the model and account it ran on, the credits it used, its version and whether it is a Keep or a Redo.

  • The exact prompt stays with the shot, so a good result can be repeated
  • Issues are written down, e.g. "duplicate character at 00:09"
  • Filter by project or status in one tap
03

Prompt library

66 tested video and image prompts, each with its example result, aspect ratio and the models it was made for. Search by brand or subject and send any prompt straight into a new shot.

  • Search: "skincare", "wrestling", "Royal Enfield"
  • Filter by Seedance, Omni, Kling, HeyGen and more
  • Add your own and they appear for the whole team
04

Character vault

Consistency is the hardest part of AI video. The vault keeps each character's face, build, outfit and voice, and each brand's logo rules, with reference images, ready to paste into a prompt.

  • Characters, brands, voices, locations and styles
  • One tap copies the locked details for a prompt
  • Reference images live with the entry
05

Costs

The tool stack, priced in ringgit, against a monthly budget. It turns credits into numbers a finance team understands: cost per delivery and cost per finished minute.

  • Switch tools on or off as subscriptions change
  • Credits by tool and by account
  • Shows how much went on redos
06

Reports

The screen I would hand to a manager or the C-suite. It writes a plain-English summary of the month, shows the headline numbers, and downloads as a one-page PDF with my name on it.

  • Projects delivered, finished minutes, on-time rate
  • Usable-first-time rate for AI shots
  • Optional benchmark against an outside agency rate

On the go

Built for the phone too.

On set or between meetings, the same studio works on a phone, with a glass dock for the main screens. Add it to the home screen and it opens like an app.

Look and feel

Liquid glass, day and night.

I art-directed the interface after Apple's liquid glass: frosted panels floating over a warm studio photo, a light that follows the cursor, and soft motion as screens change. It follows the device's light or dark setting. Drag the handle to compare.

How it was made

Directed by me.
Built with Claude.

I treated the app like any production: I wrote the brief, decided what a creative lead actually needs to track, chose the numbers management would care about, and art-directed the look. Claude wrote the code to that brief, and I reviewed every screen until it worked the way I wanted.

It runs as a private web app with a shared database, image storage and PDF export. The data on these screens is my real production log, not a mock-up.
